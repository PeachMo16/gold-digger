## what the tables actually say

Table 3 (search results, "# results <1988 / >1988") and Table 4 ("Authors and
Titles") are split into two labelled groups, verbatim headers:

**"Migraine/magnesium group"** — three articles, chosen by Swanson, not random:
1. Vosgerau 1973, *Migraine therapy with magnesium glutamate* [German] — 3 citations pre-1988, 14 after
2. Kopjas 1969, *The use of folic acid in vascular headache of the migraine type* — 0 pre, 1 after
3. Hasselmark 1987, *Effect of a carbohydrate-rich diet, low in protein-tryptophan, in classic and common migraine* — 0 pre, 7 after

**"randomly selected group"** — the by-catch of the 18-substance × 47-disease
random experiment. Lithium rows: lithium / small-cell carcinoma ×3 (1985, 1983,
1981; pre/post-1988 citations 1/0, 4/3, 0/0), lithium / melanoma ×2 (1983: 3/12;
1985: 4/6). Other rows: magnesium / psychomotor ×2, ATP / cirrhosis, folic acid /
psychomotor, phosphoenolpyruvate / reticuloendotheliosis.

Swanson's worked example in the body is Vosgerau 1973 only ("consistent and rapid
termination of migraine attacks in 10 patients … injections of magnesium
glutamate"; "cited only twice" then "about 57 citing articles" after his 1988
paper). No row in either group carries a merit comment.

## verdict on the digger's claims

- "Swanson makes no claim about lithium and cancer" — **confirmed, now with the
  tables read**: lithium rows sit in the *randomly selected group*, unevaluated,
  and the small-cell-carcinoma titles are about lithium as a chemotherapy adjunct
  (leukopenia), not lithium preventing cancer.
- "The paper does not name three leads" — **half right**. The body names one
  worked example. But the tables do single out a hand-picked three-article
  *migraine/magnesium group* — Vosgerau (Mg glutamate), Kopjas (folic acid),
  Hasselmark (carbohydrate diet) — separate from the random by-catch. So a
  reader who says "Swanson flags three neglected migraine findings" is
  defensible; a reader who says "Swanson names lithium/cancer as a neglected
  lead" is not.
