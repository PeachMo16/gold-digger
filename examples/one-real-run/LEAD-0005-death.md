# DEATH · LEAD-0005 · Swanson 2011 "neglected discoveries" — lithium/cancer thread

> **A real death file, published as-is.** One digger, 35 tool calls, a budget of 40.
> The lead was a side-finding of an *accepted* find; the dig discovered the lead
> was mis-posed at birth — the primary source never made the claim — and the
> analyst ruled it **falsified** on the truth axis. The verification dig
> ([LEAD-0005.v1.md](LEAD-0005.v1.md)) read the tables the digger could not, and
> the result forced a post-acceptance correction to the earlier find. This is what
> the pipeline is for: a machine that only ever says "I found something" cannot
> be trusted when it does.

- what was dug: Swanson DR (2011) PMC3097086 read first-hand (raw HTML text + Europe PMC XML; tables unreadable as images); follow-up literature 2011-2026 on lithium and cancer incidence/mechanism: Huang 2016 BJPsych (first-hand PDF), Anmella 2021 meta-analysis (abstract verbatim), Ge & Jakobsson 2019 Front Oncol (first-hand via fetch), Luo 2025 JAMA Netw Open (first-hand via fetch), 2018 Swanson retrospective PMC5771422 (first-hand via fetch); Martinsson 2016, Pottegård 2016, SCLC lithium RCTs, NCT02198859 at snippet level only. 35 tool calls.
- why it's barren / falsified: The lead misremembers the source. Swanson 2011 contains no claim about lithium and cancer; "lithium" appears 0 times in the body and 5 times in the reference list, as titles of 5 of 14 articles that a *random* substance×disease search (18 random substances × 47 random diseases, MEDLINE 1965-87, ≤5 citations before 1988) flagged as "neglected". He does not evaluate, discuss, or recommend any of them; his only worked case is magnesium/migraine, and the paper does not "name three leads" (the only "three" in the text are three articles that did NOT fit his search intent). Nobody in the lithium-oncology literature cites Swanson 2011 (Ge & Jakobsson 2019 do not; the 2018 Swanson retrospective mentions no lithium). Meanwhile the underlying lithium–cancer hypothesis was never neglected after 2011: register cohorts (Huang 2016 HR 0.735 [0.554-0.974] with dose-response; Martinsson 2016), a 2021 meta-analysis (five studies, 2.6 M people, pooled RR 0.94 [0.72-1.22], not significant), mechanism reviews (GSK-3/Wnt), and small trials (prostate NCT02198859 unpublished; neuroendocrine trial stopped for futility). So there is nothing here that is both Swanson's and dead.
- **what would change my mind:** (1) A readable copy of Swanson 2011 Tables 1-4 showing text — not just titles — in which Swanson comments on the lithium articles' merit (would upgrade "framing" to a real Swanson claim). (2) Any paper 2011-2026 that cites PMC3097086 as the source of a lithium/cancer hypothesis. (3) A randomized trial of lithium with a cancer-incidence or survival endpoint reporting a significant effect — that would revive the *hypothesis* as a separate, un-Swanson lead (file it as its own LEAD, not this one). (4) An updated meta-analysis after 2021 whose pooled lithium RR crosses significance in either direction.
- related areas whose future leads should trigger a re-read of this file: literature-based discovery / Arrowsmith / "neglected findings" mining; GSK-3 inhibitors in oncology (9-ING-41, AZD2858); lithium repurposing (prostate active surveillance, glioblastoma cocktails, chemo-myeloprotection); environmental lithium epidemiology (Luo 2025); bipolar-disorder cancer risk (breast RR 1.33) and confounding-by-indication in psychiatric drug cohorts; the other 13 Table-4 items (folic acid/migraine, avian REV virus/PEPCK, magnesium pemoline).
