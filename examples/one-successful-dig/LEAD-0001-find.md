# FIND · LEAD-0001 · nucleoside-modified mRNA

## three sentences

1. A 2005 *Immunity* paper showed that modified nucleosides make synthetic mRNA
   invisible enough to the innate immune system to be usable as a therapeutic —
   and was desk-rejected by *Nature* in 24 hours as "not novel."
2. It died of framing and unfashionability, not falsity: the field had closed the
   question it answered, and its senior author had been demoted for pursuing it.
3. When delivery (LNPs) and demand (a pandemic) arrived, the dormant result became
   the foundation of a vaccine platform and a 2023 Nobel Prize — the model case of
   a kill reason expiring while the paper waited.

## confidence and holes

High on every axis except **freedom**, which is the instructive one: the method
was patented by Penn and licensed commercially [4 in dig sources]. A revival that
had treated "published 2005" as "free to use" would have walked into a wall.
FTO review required before any commercial claim.

## postscript (why this example is here)

This is a known-answer test: history already ran the revival, so the pipeline's
reasoning can be checked against the outcome. What the format buys you: the death
was diagnosed from *documented rejection evidence*, not vibes; the revival needed
*named axes to flip*, not optimism; and the freedom axis caught the trap that
survives even a Nobel Prize.
