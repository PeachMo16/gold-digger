# LEAD-0001 · nucleoside-modified mRNA (Karikó & Weissman, 2005)

> **Known-answer test.** This dig was reconstructed by the pipeline's authors on
> a case that history has already graded, to show the report format doing real work.
> Every claim cites a public source; the "candidate" framing is preserved as if
> the outcome were unknown.

## find

Karikó, Buckstein, Ni & Weissman (2005), "Suppression of RNA recognition by
Toll-like receptors: the impact of nucleoside modification and the evolutionary
origin of RNA," *Immunity* 23(2):165–75. Substituting modified nucleosides
(e.g. pseudouridine) into synthetic mRNA suppresses innate-immune recognition,
removing the inflammatory response that had made mRNA look unusable as a
therapeutic. The result sat far outside the field's roadmap: mRNA was considered
too unstable, too inflammatory, and undeliverable.

## death certificate (candidate — analyst rules)

- when it stopped: the finding drew modest attention for years after 2005; the
  senior author had already been demoted at Penn (1995) for failure to attract
  mRNA funding [1]
- candidate cause: **framing + ugly** — the manuscript was desk-rejected by
  *Nature* within 24 hours as "not novel" [1]; the field's question ("is mRNA a
  viable therapeutic?") was considered closed, so a result that reopened it read
  as an answer to a dead question
- evidence: rejection history and funding starvation documented in [1], [2]

## would it die today? (candidate answer)

No. Two axes moved: **feasibility** — lipid-nanoparticle delivery matured in the
2010s, removing the "undeliverable" objection; **value** — a pandemic re-priced
"rapidly reprogrammable vaccine platform" from curiosity to necessity. The
immunogenicity objection was the very thing this paper had solved in 2005.

## analyst ruling (appended)

- cause of death: **confirmed — framing + ugly**, with a funding-starvation
  component; not falsified (the finding replicated and scaled), not timing alone
  (the bench result was producible in 2005; the *platform* was not)
- five-axis check:
  - truth ✅ replicated; foundational to licensed vaccines; Nobel Prize 2023 [3]
  - feasibility ✅ LNP delivery arrived in the 2010s
  - safety ✅ regulatory path now exists (licensed products)
  - freedom ⚠️ *candidate only* — the University of Pennsylvania patented the
    method ("Method for reducing immunogenicity of RNA," e.g. US 10,808,242 [4]);
    "published" never meant "free to operate." FTO review required — this axis is
    exactly why.
  - value ✅ live platform, live questions
- smuggling check: "immunogenicity" verified to mean the same thing in the 2005
  immunology context and the vaccine-platform context — same term, same referent; passes
- verdict: **revival candidate** (historically: revived; see FIND file)
