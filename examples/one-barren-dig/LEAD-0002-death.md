# DEATH · LEAD-0002 · polywater / anomalous water (1962–1973)

> **Known-answer test**, same discipline as LEAD-0001: reconstructed with citations
> to show what an honest death file looks like. Barren digs get written down too —
> that is house rule #1.

## what was dug

The "anomalous water" literature: Fedyakin's observations in sealed capillaries,
reported and championed by Boris Deryagin from 1962 [1]; the Western replication
wave (Pethica's confirmation published in *Nature*) [1]; the collapse, 1970–1973.

## why it's barren

Killed on the **truth axis**, which is terminal — no other axis matters after it:

- 1970–71: Denis Rousseau evaporated sweat from his own T-shirt after a handball
  game and found it matched polywater's spectrum; published in *Science*, 1971 [2]
- analysis showed the samples contaminated with organic compounds including lipids
  and phospholipids — ingredients of human perspiration [1]
- by 1973 the principal claimants, **including Deryagin himself**, disavowed the
  earlier findings [1] — the discoverer's own retraction is the strongest close a
  death file can cite

Candidate cause `ugly` or `timing` considered and rejected: the anomaly was not
unfashionable (it triggered a Cold War research race) and needed no missing
instrument. It was simply wrong. **Falsified.**

## what would change my mind

A preparation under modern contamination control reproducing the anomalous
properties, independently replicated. Five decades: none. The tripwire is stated
for form's sake; it is not expected to fire.

## related areas whose future leads should trigger a re-read of this file

Any lead claiming novel bulk phases of water in confined geometries, or
capillary-confinement anomalies generally — this file is the cautionary prior:
check the contamination story *first*.
